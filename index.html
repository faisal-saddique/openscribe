Why it's free

Because it costs me nothing to give you.

Most transcription tools charge a monthly fee to rent you a GPU with a wrapper around it. OpenScribe skips the middle step: the GPU is the one sitting unused in your own Google account.

It runs in your Google account

The notebook executes on Colab's free tier, under your login, using your quota. I never see the job, and I'm not paying for it.

Your file doesn't pass through me

Video goes from your machine — or straight from a link — into your own Colab runtime. There's no backend of mine in the path to store it, log it, or train on it.

The whole thing is 400 lines of Python

MIT licensed, readable in one sitting. Fork it, gut it, run it on your own hardware. Nothing is obfuscated and nothing phones home.

The catch, stated plainly

You do the clicking. It's a notebook, not a web app — you open it, pick a GPU runtime, and run the cells. If you want something that just takes an upload and emails you a file, this isn't it.

Before you rely on it

What it doesn't do.

Nothing here is hidden in a FAQ. These are the real limits, and for some jobs they'll be dealbreakers.

No speaker labels. Whisper doesn't do diarisation, so you get the words, not who said them. The word-level timestamps in the JSON are there if you want to bolt on a diarisation model yourself.

Colab's free tier can disconnect. Long sessions get reclaimed at busy times, and GPU availability isn't guaranteed. Past about two hours of audio, switch to the turbo model or use Colab Pro.

Whisper can invent text over silence. It's a known failure mode of the model, not of this notebook. Voice-activity filtering suppresses most of it, and the quality-check cell flags what's left — but skim the output before you trust it.

It's a notebook, not a product. No API, no team accounts, no integrations, no support queue. If you need transcription inside a business workflow, pay a real vendor — that's a fair use of money.
